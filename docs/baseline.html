01 · Write down the actual measurement

A baseline is a circuit response.

Use the e+iωt convention. The susceptibility is χ = χ′ − iχ″. The baseline has χ = 0; including nuclear susceptibility changes the same coil, not the topology. For the paper's cgs susceptibility convention:

Leff = L₀(1 + 4πηfillχ)
Zcoil = Rcoil + iωLeff
Zload = [1/Zcoil + iωCstray]−1

Writing stray capacitance as an admittance gives the correct Cstray = 0 limit: the coil remains connected. Absorption increases the real coil loss for positive polarization; dispersion changes the reactive part.

Transform the coil through the cable

Rc, Lc, Gc, and Cc are distributed parameters per meter. Use a single consistent RLGC model for both characteristic impedance and propagation:

γ = √[(Rc + iωLc)(Gc + iωCc)]
Z₀ = √[(Rc + iωLc)/(Gc + iωCc)]
Zline = Z₀ [Zload + Z₀ tanh(γℓ)] / [Z₀ + Zload tanh(γℓ)]

Choose the passive square-root branch. Cable length ℓ stays fixed during a scan. In a lossless line, zero and integer-half-wave lengths reproduce the load; a quarter-wave length gives Z₀²/Zload. Check all three cases numerically.

Include tuning and finite-source loading once

Zleg = RD + 1/(iωCtune) + Zline
u = (U/R₀) / [1/Zleg + 1/R₀ + 1/Rinput]
Vdet = G Re[u exp(iφ)] + VDC
RF source U ── R₀ ──●── R_D ── C_tune ── cable ── load
                    │                              │
                 R_input               load = (R_coil + iωL_eff)
                    │                           in parallel with C_stray
                   ground                         to ground

Detect the phase-referenced voltage at ●.

This is the nodal solution of Fig. 1 and the complex form underlying Eq. (2). There is one tuning capacitor and one application of source/input loading. The printed definitions of ZT in Eqs. (3) and (6) overlap; the code uses explicit line_input and resonator_impedance names so the capacitor cannot be counted twice. This resolves the implementation convention from the diagram without claiming a literal substitution of those inconsistent names.

Detector phase acts on the complex voltage. Do not replace phase-sensitive detection with |u| or insert an unrelated slope into the nuclear amplitude. The model supports centered linear and quadratic detector phase; this first fitting exercise keeps them fixed at zero and fits a constant phase.

02 · Understand the file before the optimizer

Units and records are part of the model.

The public example has one headerless row: a timestamp followed by 500 samples. It also has 35 formatting artifacts such as -1 .029542E-1. Ordinary numpy.loadtxt rejects these fields. The shared reader removes only the spaces before the decimal point in memory and reports every repair; it preserves all samples and leaves the CSV untouched.

Before optimization · use the tuning record

Supply what is known.
Fit only what is unknown.

A Q-meter is not an arbitrary collection of free circuit parameters. The capacitor setting, cable branch, component values, reference frequency, and detector settings often carry independent information. Give that information to the fit before looking at its residual.

Make a tuning inventory

  • Tuning capacitance: record the approximate setting, calibrated knob-to-capacitance conversion, and its uncertainty or supported limits. A knob number is not automatically a capacitance in pF.
  • Cable: record the intended integer multiple of λ/2, tuning frequency, cable type, velocity factor or measured propagation constant, physical length, and justified trim/reference-plane corrections.
  • Components and loading: use measured coil R/L, source/damping resistances, input impedance, cable losses and stray capacitance where available. Record nominal assumptions explicitly when measurements are missing.
  • Readout: preserve known RF amplitude, detector phase, gain, offset and scan units. Do not freely fit both RF voltage and readout gain when only their product is visible.
Different kinds of knowledge require different constraints

Use the known half-wave branch

β₀ = Im[γ(2πftune)]
ℓ = nπ/β₀ + δℓ,   n = 1, 2, 3, …

Supply n from the tuning record and fix or tightly bound δℓ using what is actually known. The fitter optimizes δℓ rather than searching across unrelated cable branches. Do not round the branch inferred from an unconstrained curve fit and then reuse it as independent information.

Use the wavelength inside the cable, not the vacuum wavelength: λ/2 = vphase/(2f). For a low-loss cable with independently known characteristic impedance Z₀ and velocity factor VF, useful conversions are Lc ≈ Z₀/(VF·c) and Cc ≈ 1/(Z₀·VF·c). They must be updated consistently. The implemented RLGC model computes β₀ from those cable parameters; it does not treat a new velocity factor as unrelated to them.

If the actual physical cable length is measured directly, provide cable_length_m instead and omit cable_tuning. Do not impose two contradictory length definitions. Connector/reference-plane corrections need supported tolerances; they are not a license for an arbitrary large trim.

If independent records leave several integer branches possible, compare them as separate supported hypotheses and report the ambiguity. Do not optimize the integer as a continuous parameter or declare the lowest-residual branch independently known.

Use the tuning capacitance and resonance information

At an ideal series-tuning point, Im[Zleg(ftune)] = 0. For the full modeled load this implies Ctune = 1/[2πftune Im(Zline)], when Im(Zline) is positive. The familiar isolated-coil estimate 1/(ω²L₀) is a useful check, not a replacement for cable, loss, and stray-capacitance effects.

A phase-sensitive voltage maximum need not coincide with zero resonator reactance. Use the recorded tuning procedure and frequency, not just the highest point of the measured curve, to choose the constraints.

Run a tuning-informed deuteron fit

The acquisition grid is now supplied by the owner and used by default: 500 bins, start 32.3 MHz, spacing 0.0015287 MHz, nominal center 32.7 MHz. A different documented contract can be selected with --acquisition. Explicit start, step, and frequency-source overrides must be supplied together.

Start from the complete setup template. Replace its blank tuning fields with independent records and review every nominal fixed value. The template intentionally refuses to run while required values are missing. Each active parameter must be declared fixed or fitted, with a source or explicit assumption.

python tools/fit_tuned_baseline.py examples/deuteron-baseline.csv \
  --setup my-known-setup.json \
  --starts 12 --output-dir local-results/tuning-informed-baseline-fit

The setup contains parameters and, optionally, cable_tuning with half_wave_multiple, reference_hz, and source. When using that branch convention, declare cable_delta_length_m in place of cable_length_m. All circuit fields use the units in their names; detector phase is in radians and readout gain/offset remain in recorded units.

Objective = Σi[(yfit,i − yi)/σi]²
+ Σk[(θk − θmeasured,k)/σθ,k]²

With Gaussian hardware constraints, provide noise_sigma_recorded as a measured standard deviation or a 500-bin array. The report separates the data and constraint contributions. This implementation assumes diagonal data errors and independent Gaussian constraints; correlated uncertainties require a corresponding extension. Without noise estimates, fixed values and bounds still work with unweighted least squares, but no likelihood or parameter-error claim is made.

The report saves the full setup, sources, fixed/fitted status, bounds, constraint means/uncertainties, resolved physical length, free-parameter count, all starting-point solutions, and reconstruction coefficients. If everything is fixed, it evaluates the circuit without running an optimizer.

The implementation is fit_tuned_baseline.py. Reconstruct its saved prediction with reconstruct_fit(f_hz, report["fits"][0]) from that module; this retains any specified frequency-dependent detector phase. The separate fit_baseline.py implementation illustrates variable projection; its broad built-in search bounds are numerical assumptions, not known deuteron settings.

Reproduce the displayed measured example

The confirmed acquisition is loaded automatically. This exploratory comparison fits tuning capacitance, cable length and stray capacitance, then solves the three readout coordinates. Its numerical bounds are 0.2–600 pF, 3–5 m and 0–400 pF; they are not measured component tolerances. Known frequency information is fixed. Other fixed components are nominal assumptions, clearly listed in the table above.

The fitted length is about 1.35443 nominal half-waves, not an independently known integer branch. Replace this unrestricted length search with the actual branch/trim record when available. The public provenance includes all 24 starting-point candidates, bound status, and the weakly conditioned Jacobian.

Use the setup-driven workflow after confirming the frequency grid. Fixed measurements are excluded from the optimizer; only declared unknowns vary within supported bounds. Evaluate the cable propagation and capacitor impedance at the actual deuteron frequencies throughout the sweep.

Separate readout coordinates from circuit coordinates

For constant phase, write the complex node voltage u as:

yfit(f) = a Re(u) + b Im(u) + d
a = A cos φ₀,   b = −A sin φ₀

A, phase, and offset are the same three readout degrees of freedom as a, b, and d. They are not additional unknowns. If all three are unconstrained, linear least squares can eliminate them at each circuit step. If phase, gain, or offset has a measured value or constraint, the setup-driven fitter keeps that information in its explicit readout parameterization.

Keep all starting-point candidates, convergence flags, bound hits, and objective values. Multiple nearly equivalent solutions reveal weak identifiability. A small residual does not turn a nominal component seed into a measured hardware value.

All 500 bins enter this first workflow; no region is silently masked or smoothed. Without a measured data-error model, do not report reduced χ² or calibrated parameter-error bars.

04 · Let the residual challenge the fit

The overlay is not the whole result.

The displayed fit has whole-scan RMS 6.9156 × 10⁻⁵ recorded units (0.0281% of peak-to-peak range). Its largest absolute residual is 8.6451 × 10⁻⁴ recorded units at the final bin, and lag-one residual correlation is 0.399. Every sample remains included. The scaled shape-Jacobian condition is about 4.95 × 10⁴, so a close curve match does not establish unique component values.

Before accepting a generator configuration

  • Retain every measured bin and both endpoints in the first comparison.
  • Look for localized features, settling artifacts, smooth drift, and correlation.
  • Check bound hits and variation across nearly equivalent starting-point solutions.
  • Inspect the scaled Jacobian and acknowledge weak parameter combinations.
  • If a signal region or settling bins must later be excluded, record the mask and show those bins separately.
  • Validate on independent acquisitions, not just other bins from the fitted sweep.

Residual structure can include signal, model mismatch, drift, and acquisition artifacts. It is not automatically electronic noise. Baseline RMS is not a polarization-accuracy metric.

06 · From one fit to a training population

Match a family of deuteron measurements.

One baseline scan cannot establish a distribution of hardware configurations or separate noise from drift. Keep the confirmed frequency contract, establish the voltage scale, constrain degenerate components independently, characterize repeated-scan noise, and collect representative operating periods before estimating joint training distributions.

The main generator already uses a circuit reference of 32.68 MHz. Its nominal components, synthetic sweep, noise, and TE calibration are teaching settings, not measurements inferred from the supplied baseline. A valid baseline fit alone cannot calibrate the nuclear susceptibility or polarization scale.

Next, fit the complex nuclear response with independent calibration, validate signal-to-baseline scales, and check the full generator against development spectra. Reserve independent acquisitions for final estimator evaluation.

01 · Write down the actual measurement

A baseline is a circuit response.

Use the e+iωt convention. The susceptibility is χ = χ′ − iχ″. The baseline has χ = 0; including nuclear susceptibility changes the same coil, not the topology. For the paper's cgs susceptibility convention:

Leff = L₀(1 + 4πηfillχ)
Zcoil = Rcoil + iωLeff
Zload = [1/Zcoil + iωCstray]−1

Writing stray capacitance as an admittance gives the correct Cstray = 0 limit: the coil remains connected. Absorption increases the real coil loss for positive polarization; dispersion changes the reactive part.

Transform the coil through the cable

Rc, Lc, Gc, and Cc are distributed parameters per meter. Use a single consistent RLGC model for both characteristic impedance and propagation:

γ = √[(Rc + iωLc)(Gc + iωCc)]
Z₀ = √[(Rc + iωLc)/(Gc + iωCc)]
Zline = Z₀ [Zload + Z₀ tanh(γℓ)] / [Z₀ + Zload tanh(γℓ)]

Choose the passive square-root branch. Cable length ℓ stays fixed during a scan. In a lossless line, zero and integer-half-wave lengths reproduce the load; a quarter-wave length gives Z₀²/Zload. Check all three cases numerically.

Include tuning and finite-source loading once

Zleg = RD + 1/(iωCtune) + Zline
u = (U/R₀) / [1/Zleg + 1/R₀ + 1/Rinput]
Vdet = G Re[u exp(iφ)] + VDC
RF source U ── R₀ ──●── R_D ── C_tune ── cable ── load
                    │                              │
                 R_input               load = (R_coil + iωL_eff)
                    │                           in parallel with C_stray
                   ground                         to ground

Detect the phase-referenced voltage at ●.

This is the nodal solution of Fig. 1 and the complex form underlying Eq. (2). There is one tuning capacitor and one application of source/input loading. The printed definitions of ZT in Eqs. (3) and (6) overlap; the code uses explicit line_input and resonator_impedance names so the capacitor cannot be counted twice. This resolves the implementation convention from the diagram without claiming a literal substitution of those inconsistent names.

Detector phase acts on the complex voltage. Do not replace phase-sensitive detection with |u| or insert an unrelated slope into the nuclear amplitude. The model supports centered linear and quadratic detector phase; this first fitting exercise keeps them fixed at zero and fits a constant phase.

Before optimization · use the tuning record

Supply what is known.
Fit only what is unknown.

A Q-meter is not an arbitrary collection of free circuit parameters. The capacitor setting, cable branch, component values, reference frequency, and detector settings often carry independent information. Give that information to the fit before looking at its residual.

Make a tuning inventory

  • Tuning capacitance: record the approximate setting, calibrated knob-to-capacitance conversion, and its uncertainty or supported limits. A knob number is not automatically a capacitance in pF.
  • Cable: record the intended integer multiple of λ/2, tuning frequency, cable type, velocity factor or measured propagation constant, physical length, and justified trim/reference-plane corrections.
  • Components and loading: use measured coil R/L, source/damping resistances, input impedance, cable losses and stray capacitance where available. Record nominal assumptions explicitly when measurements are missing.
  • Readout: preserve known RF amplitude, detector phase, gain, offset and scan units. Do not freely fit both RF voltage and readout gain when only their product is visible.

Apply this information before fitting

The owner confirms a cable very close to one half-wave: 3.580 m at 32.7 MHz. The paper, Sec. 2 explicitly describes integer half-wave tuning and a velocity factor near 0.78. This preset fixes n = 1 and the propagation scale, allowing only a provisional ±3% trim (±10.74 cm). That is a working interpretation of “a few percent,” not a measured uncertainty. Do not widen it to compensate for other model errors.

β₀ = Im[γ(2πftune)]
ℓ = nπ/β₀ + δℓ,   n = 1, 2, 3, …

Supply n from the tuning record and fix or tightly bound δℓ using what is actually known. The fitter optimizes δℓ rather than searching across unrelated cable branches. Do not round the branch inferred from an unconstrained curve fit and then reuse it as independent information.

Make propagation agree with the known half-wave

h = 3.580 m,   β₀ = π/h
vphase/c = 2f₀h/c = 0.7809803

The cable constants must agree with that electrical wavelength. For the explicit nominal assumptions Zlc = √(Lc/Cc) = 50 Ω, Rc = 3.43 Ω/m and Gc = 0, solve the exact lossy propagation relation:

Cc = 2β₀² / [ω₀ √(Rc² + 4β₀² Zlc²)]
Lc = Zlc² Cc

This gives Lc = 213.3918 nH/m and Cc = 85.3567 pF/m. The exact complex Z₀ is approximately 50.0382 − 1.9543i Ω; 50 Ω denotes nominal √(L/C), not a purely real lossy impedance. The calibrated_cable_lc helper derives both constants; the setup's half_wave_length_m check rejects inconsistent propagation. See the derivation and tuning record. Nominal impedance and loss still need independent characterization.

Use the wavelength inside the cable, not the vacuum wavelength: λ/2 = vphase/(2f). For a low-loss cable with independently known characteristic impedance Z₀ and velocity factor VF, useful conversions are Lc ≈ Z₀/(VF·c) and Cc ≈ 1/(Z₀·VF·c). They must be updated consistently. The implemented RLGC model computes β₀ from those cable parameters; it does not treat a new velocity factor as unrelated to them.

If the actual physical cable length is measured directly, provide cable_length_m instead and omit cable_tuning. Do not impose two contradictory length definitions. Connector/reference-plane corrections need supported tolerances; they are not a license for an arbitrary large trim.

If independent records leave several integer branches possible, compare them as separate supported hypotheses and report the ambiguity. Do not optimize the integer as a continuous parameter or declare the lowest-residual branch independently known.

Use the tuning capacitance and resonance information

At an ideal series-tuning point, Im[Zleg(ftune)] = 0. For the full modeled load this implies Ctune = 1/[2πftune Im(Zline)], when Im(Zline) is positive. The familiar isolated-coil estimate 1/(ω²L₀) is a useful check, not a replacement for cable, loss, and stray-capacitance effects.

A phase-sensitive voltage maximum need not coincide with zero resonator reactance. Use the recorded tuning procedure and frequency, not just the highest point of the measured curve, to choose the constraints.

Objective = Σi[(yfit,i − yi)/σi]²
+ Σk[(θk − θmeasured,k)/σθ,k]²

With Gaussian hardware constraints, provide noise_sigma_recorded as a measured standard deviation or a 500-bin array. The report separates the data and constraint contributions. Capacitance search intervals are 10–2000 pF and 0–400 pF; those are numerical bounds, not measured component tolerances. Use the actual capacitor setting when available.

All 24 starts remain inside the cable interval. The solution presses against its upper end and the 400 pF stray-capacitance limit. That is a warning about the remaining assumptions, not evidence that the installed cable measures 3.6874 m. The public JSON retains every candidate, convergence flag, active-bound mask and explicit near-bound warning.

Separate readout coordinates from circuit coordinates

yfit(f) = a Re(u) + b Im(u) + d
a = A cos φ₀,   b = −A sin φ₀

A, phase and offset are the same three unknowns as a,b,d. With profile: true for all three, linear least squares eliminates them at every circuit step. There are six fitted degrees of freedom, not three or nine. If the detector has a known frequency-dependent phase, rotate u by that phase before forming the linear basis; the implementation and reconstruction retain it.

If gain, phase or offset has an independent fixed value or constraint, use the explicit fixed/bounded readout mode. Do not fit RF amplitude and gain simultaneously: their product is not separately identifiable.

The optimizer scales bounded coordinates to [0,1] and uses three-point finite differences. Its reported Jacobian covers the three nonlinear coordinates after readout elimination, not the full parameter covariance. A near-bound warning is issued within 10⁻⁵ of a declared interval width even when the optimizer's own active-bound mask is zero.

All 500 bins are included without smoothing or masking. Without a measured error model, do not report reduced χ², calibrated error bars or polarization accuracy from the baseline residual.

04 · Let the residual challenge the fit

The overlay is not the whole result.

The constrained fit has whole-scan RMS 8.1195 × 10⁻⁴ recorded units (0.3296% of peak-to-peak range). The largest residual is approximately 3.5850 × 10⁻³ at the first bin, and lag-one correlation is 0.9958. Every sample remains included. The scaled, profiled shape-Jacobian condition is about 5.42 × 10⁴; it does not supply physical parameter uncertainties.

Do not accept this as a hardware calibration

The +3% length trim and 400 pF stray-capacitance bounds are reached. The effective tuning capacitor is about 49.92 pF, with gain about 1.606 × 10⁴ recorded units/V and offset about −194.6 recorded units. These large compensating coefficients and smooth residuals challenge the remaining nominal components and constant-phase assumption. The assumed 30 nH coil suggests an isolated-coil tuning scale near 790 pF, so compare the actual capacitor setting and full resonator reactance before accepting the fit. Keep the cable constraint; obtain independent component and detector information.

Before accepting a generator configuration

  • Retain every measured bin and both endpoints in the first comparison.
  • Look for localized features, settling artifacts, smooth drift, and correlation.
  • Check bound hits and variation across nearly equivalent starting-point solutions.
  • Inspect the scaled Jacobian and acknowledge weak parameter combinations.
  • If a signal region or settling bins must later be excluded, record the mask and show those bins separately.
  • Validate on independent acquisitions, not just other bins from the fitted sweep.

Residual structure can include signal, model mismatch, drift, and acquisition artifacts. It is not automatically electronic noise. Baseline RMS is not a polarization-accuracy metric.
